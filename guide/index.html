How to read a fundus photo in 5 steps

Fundus photos come up in exams and OSCE stations. Use the same order every time and you'll never freeze in front of one again.

Tap the numbers

Each number is one step. Tap to jump to it.

STEP 1

Orient yourself

  • Find the optic disc first: the bright yellow-orange circle.
  • The disc sits on the nasal side, and the macula is about 2 disc diameters temporal to it.
  • So in a standard photo, a disc on the right usually means a right eye.
Say it out loud in the OSCE: "This is a fundus photograph of the right eye."
STEP 2

Optic disc: the 3 Cs

  • Colour: healthy discs are pink-orange. Pale = think atrophy.
  • Contour: margins should be sharp. Blurred or swollen = think disc swelling (e.g. papilledema).
  • Cup: estimate the cup-to-disc ratio. A large or asymmetric cup = think glaucoma.
Most normal discs have a cup-to-disc ratio below 0.5.
STEP 3

Vessels

  • Arteries are thinner and lighter; veins are wider and darker.
  • Look at the crossings: is the vein being pinched (AV nicking)?
  • Look for abnormal new vessels, especially on or near the disc.
Follow the vessels outward from the disc, one arcade at a time.
STEP 4

Macula

  • The darker area temporal to the disc. The fovea is its center.
  • Look for drusen, exudates, haemorrhages or a cherry-red spot.
  • Changes here matter most for central vision.
Never skip the macula. Small findings here are easy to miss.
STEP 5

Background & periphery

  • Red lesions: dots, blots, flame-shaped haemorrhages, microaneurysms.
  • Yellow/white lesions: hard exudates, cotton wool spots, drusen.
  • Check all four quadrants, then the far edges.
Describe what you see first (colour, shape, location), then name it.
